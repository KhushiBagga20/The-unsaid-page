The Dust on the Window Sill

Editor's Pick • Short Reflection

I noticed it this morning, a fine layer of grey coating the white paint of the sill. It’s strange how dust accumulates; it is the physical evidence of time passing, of skin shedding, of the world slowly turning while we are busy looking at screens or worrying about tomorrow.

I didn't wipe it away immediately. There was something strangely comforting about it. It reminded me of the house I grew up in, where the sunlight would catch the floating motes in the afternoon, turning the living room into a cathedral of light and silence. We are so obsessed with cleanliness, with the new, with the pristine. But there is a history in dust.

It made me think of all the things we leave behind when the seasons change. The sweaters packed into boxes, the books we meant to read but never opened, the conversations that trailed off into silence. We move forward, always forward, but the dust settles on everything we leave in our wake.

Eventually, I took a cloth and wiped the sill clean. The wood gleamed, bright and new. But for a moment, I missed the grey. I missed the quiet proof that time had been here, resting its heavy head against the glass.
