The Last Train to Paris

Published by Julian V. on November 4th

The steam rose from the platform like a ghost refusing to leave the haunting grounds. It was 11:04 PM, and the station was nearly empty, save for an old man reading a newspaper that was three days out of date.

She tightened her scarf. The wool was scratchy against her neck, but it was the only thing holding the cold at bay. She checked her watch again. The second hand ticked with an agonizing slowness, chopping time into small, digestible pieces that she couldn't quite swallow.

"You don't have to go," he had said, standing in the doorway of their apartment, his silhouette cut out by the hallway light. But she did. They both knew that staying was a luxury they could no longer afford. The tickets in her pocket felt heavy, like stones.

The whistle blew. A sharp, piercing shriek that cut through the silence of the night. The train was here.
